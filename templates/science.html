How to get there

Follow these steps

  1. Give both clusters a positive charge. Which way do the arrows point? Like charges push apart: the force is repulsive.
  2. Now make them attract. What has to be true about the two charges? Opposite charges pull together: the force is attractive.
  3. Pin the force, then halve the distance. How many times bigger is the force? Half the distance gives 4× the force. That is an inverse-square law: F ∝ 1/r².
  4. Pin the force, then double one cluster’s charge. Keep the distance the same. Double the charge, 2× the force. Force grows with q₁ × q₂.
  5. Add neutrons to a charged cluster. Does the force change? No. Neutrons have no charge, so they add mass but no electric force.
  6. Balance one cluster’s protons and electrons. What happens? A neutral cluster (q = 0) feels no electrostatic force at all.

The math, live

F = k · |q1 · q2| / r2

    Force vs. distance

    Check your answer to the question

    Claim. The electric force between two charged particles gets bigger as their charges get bigger, and much smaller as they move apart. Like charges repel and opposite charges attract.

    Evidence. Doubling one charge doubled the force. Halving the distance made the force 4 times bigger. Two positive clusters pushed apart, a positive and a negative cluster pulled together, and a neutral cluster felt no force. Adding neutrons changed nothing.

    Reasoning. Coulomb’s law, F = k·|q₁q₂|/r², says force is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance. Only protons (+1) and electrons (−1) carry charge, so only they change the force.
